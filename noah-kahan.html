Noah Kahan — The Folk Singer Who Made Homesickness a Genre

"Stick Season" turned a Vermont singer-songwriter's specific, small-town ache into one of the decade's most unlikely mass phenomena.

Strafford, Vermont — Breakthrough: 2022

Chapter I

From Pop Hopeful to Folk's Breakout Voice

Noah Kahan started his career aiming for a more conventional pop lane before pivoting toward the folk-influenced, New England-specific songwriting that actually broke him through — a reminder that the sound an artist is "supposed" to make and the one that connects aren't always the same.

"Stick Season" (2022) turned small-town homesickness, seasonal depression, and small-talk anxiety into an entire aesthetic, resonating especially with listeners who'd never heard those specific feelings named so plainly in a song before.

Chapter II

Turning a Following Into a Movement

Kahan has been unusually open about his own mental health, and built the Busyhead Foundation to fund mental health resources for fans attending his shows — a rare case of a musician's advocacy work becoming as central to his public identity as the music itself.

2026's "The Great Divide" and an extensive summer tour suggest the "Stick Season" moment was a foundation, not a ceiling — he's still one of the most in-demand touring folk-pop acts working.

Golden

A fictional girl group's "I want" song became a real Grammy winner — and the reason is that its central lie-turned-truth wasn't fictional at all.

HUNTR/X — EJAE, Audrey Nuna, REI AMI Released July 2025 Grammy, Best Song for Visual Media

I was a ghost. I was alone. Now I'm done hiding.

The "I want" song, done right

In musical theatre terms, "Golden" is what's known as an "I want" song — the number where a character stops describing their world and starts naming what they're actually after. In KPop Demon Hunters, that character is Rumi, the half-demon member of the fictional group HUNTR/X, and what she wants is simple to say and hard to do: to stop living two lives and finally be seen as whole.

Written by Ejae, Mark Sonnenblick, 24, Ido, and Teddy Park, and performed by EJAE, Audrey Nuna and Rei Ami as the voices of Rumi, Mira, and Zoey, the song functions inside the film as a literal weapon — the group releases it early to trigger a protective barrier called the Golden Honmoon. But its power was never really about the plot mechanics. It's about the very ordinary, very real experience the lyric describes underneath the fantasy: hiding a part of yourself for so long that you start to forget which version is the mask.

A duality the song refuses to resolve quietly

Rumi's arc — split between a human world and a demon inheritance she's spent her life concealing — gives "Golden" a specificity most glow-up anthems don't bother with. The verses don't gloss over the cost of hiding: being called a problem child, feeling unable to find her own place, years spent performing a version of herself that fit other people's comfort rather than her own truth. The chorus doesn't erase that history. It just refuses to let it be the ending.

That refusal — insisting on shining rather than continuing to apologize for existing loudly — is what elevates "Golden" from a catchy needle-drop into something closer to an anthem. It resonated specifically because so many listeners, K-pop fans or not, recognized their own version of Rumi's split: the self they show the world, and the self they've learned to keep quiet.

Why a cartoon anthem outran real pop stars

Part of "Golden"'s staying power is structural — the song builds like an actual pop performance, starting hushed and introspective before erupting into a full, layered chorus built for a stadium, not a soundtrack. But the deeper reason is that KPop Demon Hunters refused to make its heroines conventionally perfect. None of the three members of HUNTR/X fit the polished mold real K-pop idols are so often pressed into — and "Golden" makes the case, loudly, that the mold was always the problem, not the girls who didn't fit it.

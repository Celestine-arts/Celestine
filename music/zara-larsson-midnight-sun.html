Electropop — Grammy nominated

Midnight Sun

A song built entirely out of one feeling: the specific freedom of a Swedish summer night that never has to end.

Zara Larsson Midnight Sun Released June 13, 2025

If Zara Larsson had to be one of her own songs, she's said, she'd be this one.

Writing a night that isn't allowed to end

Larsson wrote "Midnight Sun" while working with her longtime collaborator MNEK in Jamaica, inspired — almost too literally — by an enormous beaver moon they watched together that lit the whole sky in a faint rainbow. That image of an impossible, glowing night became the seed for the entire album: a record she wanted to feel like one long Swedish summer evening, the kind where the sun never fully sets and time stops mattering.

The title borrows directly from the real astronomical phenomenon of the midnight sun, a fixture of Scandinavian summers where daylight stretches deep into the night. Larsson has said she wanted to write specifically into her own pop heritage — a song that could only have come from someone who grew up watching those endless Nordic evenings.

It's the sense of being free from responsibilities and the mundane things.Zara Larsson, on the feeling behind the song

A summer romance with no clock on it

Structurally, "Midnight Sun" blends electropop, drum and bass, and Eurodance into something that critics have described as teetering between euphoric and delirious — appropriate for a song about a night that seems to bend the rules of time. The lyrics move through skinny-dipping, driving too fast, initials pressed into sand: small, specific images of a romance that only works because neither person is watching the clock.

It's a simpler emotional proposition than most of the songs built around heartbreak or self-doubt — which, notably, make up much of the rest of the Midnight Sun album, an otherwise introspective record about ambition and public scrutiny. This single track is the release valve: pure, uncomplicated joy, borrowed from one very specific, very real Swedish summer feeling.

Why a song about nothing happening resonates so widely

There's no conflict in "Midnight Sun," no betrayal or ultimatum — which is rare for a song this size to get away with. It works because the feeling it's chasing is universal in a different way: the memory of one perfect, unhurried night that you wished could have lasted. Larsson isn't asking you to relate to a specific heartbreak. She's asking you to remember what it felt like before there was anything to break.

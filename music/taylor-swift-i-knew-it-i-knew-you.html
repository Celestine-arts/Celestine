for Toy Story 5

I Knew It,
I Knew You

A song about the specific joy of recognizing someone — written for an animated cowgirl doll, and somehow about everyone who's ever come back.

Taylor Swift with Jack Antonoff Released June 5, 2026

Every "Toy Story" film eventually asks the same question — what happens to the thing you loved when time moves on without asking permission. Swift's contribution to the fifth film doesn't answer it so much as it remembers what it felt like the first time love came back anyway.

Written for Jessie, shaped by a lifetime of Toy Story

Swift confirmed her involvement in "Toy Story 5" on June 1, 2026, after a week of easter eggs, and released the single four days later. She has described a personal thread running underneath the assignment: she's been, in her own words, a Toy Story kid since she was five years old, and writing something for Jessie — the cowgirl doll defined by having been left behind and loved again — felt like "a musical departure and coming home at the same time."

Co-written and co-produced with her longtime collaborator Jack Antonoff, the track marks a deliberate return to a more countrified sound after years of denser, atmospheric production — pared-back guitar, her voice sitting close to the mic, layered vocals only arriving as the song builds toward its climax. It mirrors, quite literally, the shape of remembering something: quiet at first, then everything arriving at once.

"Writing this song felt like a musical departure and coming home at the same time."Taylor Swift, on Instagram, June 2026

A door built to open twice

The song's structure is a reunion in miniature: a narrator who assumed a relationship — romantic, platonic, or simply a piece of someone's own childhood — was over, only to find it waiting, unchanged, in a window's worth of afternoon light. Swift has since confirmed on country radio that several lines were written with deliberate double meanings, built to work as both a specific beat in the film's plot and a more universal memory of nostalgia the listener supplies themselves.

That doubling is the song's real craft. A soundtrack cue has to serve a two-hour film in under three minutes; a Taylor Swift single has to survive completely outside of it. "I Knew It, I Knew You" does both by refusing to over-specify — it never quite says whether the "you" is a person, a childhood, or a version of yourself you thought you'd outgrown.

Why a doll's song lands like a memory

It would have been easy to write something purely functional — a soundtrack single engineered for a trailer moment and nothing more. Instead, the song leans into a very specific, very ordinary kind of joy: not the drama of falling in love, but the quieter relief of realizing someone you thought you'd lost still recognizes you too, and you them.

That's a smaller emotion than most Swift singles reach for, and it's exactly why it works as well outside the movie as inside it. Everyone has their own version of the window, the smile, the "it's been a while." The song doesn't need Toy Story to make sense. It just borrows Jessie's story to give a very common feeling a very specific shape.

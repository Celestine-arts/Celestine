Timothée Chalamet — The One Who Made Prestige Cinema Feel Young Again

Somewhere between Paul Atreides and Bob Dylan, he became the rare young star that both arthouse critics and teenage fans claim as their own.

New York City, USA — Born December 27, 1995

Chapter I

The Call Me By Your Name Effect

Timothée Chalamet's breakout came with 2017's "Call Me By Your Name," a performance that earned him an Oscar nomination at 22 and immediately marked him as a different kind of young star — one critics compared to a young Al Pacino or James Dean, rather than another rising teen idol.

He spent the years after building a filmography almost engineered to prove that comparison right: "Lady Bird," "Little Women," "Beautiful Boy" — small, character-driven films with serious directors, even as his fame kept scaling up around him.

Chapter II

Dune, and Becoming a Blockbuster Lead

Chalamet's casting as Paul Atreides in Denis Villeneuve's "Dune" (2021) and its sequel "Dune: Part Two" (2024) proved he could anchor a massive science-fiction franchise without losing the art-house credibility he'd built — a rare combination in modern Hollywood.

By the time "Wonka" became a $600-million global hit in 2023, he'd effectively closed the gap between "festival darling" and "movie star" that most actors spend a career trying to bridge.

Chapter III

Bob Dylan, Ping-Pong, and an Oscar Season to Remember

In 2025, Chalamet spent years preparing to play Bob Dylan in James Mangold's "A Complete Unknown" — learning guitar and Dylan's singing voice from scratch — and earned a second Oscar nomination for it.

Then, almost as if to prove his range again, he won Best Actor at both the Critics Choice Awards and the Golden Globes in January 2026 for "Marty Supreme," playing an ambitious real-life table-tennis champion — a performance about as far from Bob Dylan as an actor could choose.

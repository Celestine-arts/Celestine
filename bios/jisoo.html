Artist Bio · BLACKPINK

Jisoo — The Visual Who Set Her Own Pace

Of the four, she was always the one least in a hurry — and a decade later, that patience looks a lot like strategy.

Seoul, South Korea — Debut: 2016

Chapter I

The Quiet One in a Loud Debut

Kim Ji-soo trained for years inside YG Entertainment before BLACKPINK debuted in August 2016 with "Whistle" and "Boombayah," arriving as the group's lead vocalist and visual — the member the camera found first, even when she wasn't the one reaching for it.

Where her groupmates built loud, unmistakable stage personas early, Jisoo's appeal was subtler: a steadiness on camera that read as confidence rather than ambition. It made her an easy pick for endorsements and magazine covers long before she released a note of solo music.

Chapter II

The Actress Nobody Expected So Soon

In 2021, Jisoo took a lead role in the JTBC/Disney+ drama "Snowdrop," a politically charged 1980s-set thriller — a genuinely risky choice for an idol, given the show's controversial subject matter drew backlash before it even aired. She stayed in the role anyway, and the performance became a turning point in how seriously Korean audiences took her acting.

She's continued down that path since, including a role in the drama "Newtopia," treating acting less like a side project and more like a second career built in parallel to music.

Chapter III

Blissoo, and Choosing the Slow Build

When BLACKPINK's members began branching into solo ventures, Jisoo took the least conventional route: rather than signing to a big label for her solo career, she founded her own agency, BLISSOO, giving her direct control over her own music and image.

Her solo project, the mini-album "AMORTAGE," followed in that same spirit — smaller, more deliberate, less engineered for an immediate viral moment than some of her groupmates' releases. It's a fitting move for an artist whose whole career has quietly bet on staying power over speed.

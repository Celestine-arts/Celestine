Artist Bio · BLACKPINK

Rosé — The Balladeer Who Went Pop Without Losing the Ache

A trained vocalist with a folk-singer's instinct for melancholy, who somehow also co-wrote one of the biggest pop songs of the decade.

Auckland, New Zealand — Debut: 2016

Chapter I

The Voice That Was Always the Point

Park Chae-young, born and raised in Australia and New Zealand before moving to Korea to train, became BLACKPINK's main vocalist — the member most consistently singled out for pure vocal ability, in a group not exactly short on talent.

Even inside BLACKPINK's high-energy discography, her tone carried a kind of restraint — a hint of the acoustic, singer-songwriter instinct that would later define her solo work.

Chapter II

"APT." and an Unlikely Global Duet

In late 2024, Rosé released her debut solo album "rosie," but it was the single "APT.", a collaboration with Bruno Mars, that turned into a genuine global phenomenon — a rare case of a K-pop solo artist landing a crossover hit that dominated charts well outside the usual K-pop audience.

It reframed her, almost overnight, from "BLACKPINK's vocalist" to a solo pop force in her own right, on her own terms.

Chapter III

The Black Label, and Staying Herself

Unlike some of her groupmates, Rosé kept her solo career under BLACKPINK's own agency umbrella, working through The Black Label rather than founding an independent company — a quieter, less headline-grabbing choice that fits an artist who has always let the songs make the noise instead.

With BLACKPINK's group activities and her own solo momentum now running side by side, Rosé's story is still being written in real time — which, for an artist this early into a solo career this successful, is exactly where you'd want to be.

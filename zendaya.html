Zendaya — The Multihyphenate Who Never Repeats a Role

From a Disney Channel dancer to the youngest two-time Emmy winner in her category, and this is still just the middle of the story.

Oakland, California — Born September 1, 1996

Chapter I

From Shake It Up to Rue Bennett

Zendaya started as a Disney Channel star on "Shake It Up," the kind of beginning that typically boxes a young actor in for years. She broke out of it early and decisively with HBO's "Euphoria" in 2019, playing Rue Bennett with a rawness that had little in common with her earlier Disney work.

The role made her the youngest actress to win two Primetime Emmys for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Drama Series — a distinction that reset how the industry saw her, permanently.

Chapter II

Blockbusters Without Losing the Range

Alongside "Euphoria," Zendaya built a parallel career as MJ in Marvel's "Spider-Man" trilogy and as Chani in Denis Villeneuve's "Dune" films — proving she could anchor franchise-scale blockbusters without stepping away from prestige television.

Her lead, producing role in 2024's "Challengers" pushed that further still, showing she could also carry an original adult drama on star power alone, not franchise recognition.

Chapter III

A Single Year, Four Films

2026 brought an unusually stacked slate even by her standards: the A24 film "The Drama," a third season of "Euphoria," Christopher Nolan's epic "The Odyssey," "Spider-Man: Brand New Day," and "Dune: Part Three" — five major releases inside a single year.

Off screen, 2026 also brought public confirmation of her marriage to longtime partner and "Spider-Man" co-star Tom Holland, a relationship that began on the set of the first film they made together back in 2017.
